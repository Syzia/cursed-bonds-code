Jujutsu Kaisen: Cursed Bonds · storyboard for review · 29 Sep 2026

Arc One呪縛 第一章

The plan for Arc One before anything is built: the story act by act, the missions, grades, combat, cursed tools, the new UI, and what ComfyUI can do to make it feel alive. Nothing here is implemented yet. The seven questions at the end need your answer first.

見My take

I agree with all of it. The game has strong bones. It plays like "a PNG story" for fixable reasons.

What's there

A calendar of story beats up to the Kyoto event on day 22, a bond system with written hangouts at three tiers, eight character threads, a real turn-based combat engine built on reading a curse's tell, 13 characters with six expressions each, and 24 painted campus backgrounds.

Why it feels flat

No scene uses the expressions we made. Side portraits never render. Characters vanish while you talk to them. Only 3 fights use the combat engine, and HP is never restored, so every fight after the prologue starts at zero. The HUD shows about 12 numbers at all times. There is no audio at all.

Season 1

A good spine. The original plot already in the game fits it well. Wakuri, the curse user inside the Grading Office who files curses one grade low, is exactly what went wrong at the detention center in the show. I'd keep him as the hidden hand and let Season 1's beats be what you see.

First person

Yes. Kaito is on screen in about 40 scenes today. Taking him off makes every other character look at you. His new art moves to the journal, combat and a few big moments.

Scope

This is weeks of work, not days. I'd build the foundation first (UI, grades, the mission system, first person), then combat, then the story one act at a time.

任Utahime's mission board

The side missions between story beats. They replace today's text-only patrol.

  • Graded. You can take missions at your grade. With a senior partner you can take one grade higher.
  • Built from parts: a place, a curse and a twist. For example a Grade 3 missing-persons case in a tunnel where the curse only moves in the dark.
  • Bring someone. Your partner fights with you, adds their support move and gains bond.
  • Rewards: progress toward promotion, yen for supplies, bond, and sometimes a cursed tool.
  • Misgraded missions. Now and then a Grade 3 job hides a Grade 2 curse. That's Wakuri at work, and it becomes a clue.

Special grade covers curses and curse users too. Sukuna, the King of Curses, and Geto, whose body Kenjaku now wears, sit at the very top of the story, and Sukuna is the final villain, in the end taking Yuji's body for good. His own body is on campus. On the mountain ridge above the school, Sukuna's mummified body sits cross-legged as if meditating, its fingers missing, sealed inside a veil Jujutsu High keeps up day and night. It replaces the casket in the storehouse. Kenjaku is the one Wakuri really serves. Yuki Tsukumo, the third special-grade sorcerer, arrives in a later arc.

What your grade decides

  • Which missions Utahime will give you.
  • Your cursed energy reserve, and how far your stats can grow.
  • What the armory will release to you.
  • Promotion needs merit from missions, a promotion mission, and for Grade 1, two recommendations.

戦Combat and powers

The engine at the heart of it is good: each curse shows a tell, and you answer it (brace a heavy blow, step aside from a fast one, break a grab, close on a ranged attack). I'd keep that and build on it.

Fix first

  • HP is never restored, so every fight after the prologue starts at zero.
  • The prologue fight can't end if you play well (900 HP, no retreat).
  • Some technique moves deal damage and also block the hit, which removes the choice.
  • Every ally hits the same way, using your stats.
  • In the first Nade mission, losing gives more bond than winning. Infirmary rest and storehouse tools cost no time and can be repeated forever.
  • The Sun Mall, the courtyard curse and patrols are menus, not fights.

Add

Black Flash

A timing meter on your strike with a tiny window. Hit it and you deal 2.5 times the damage, refill cursed energy, and the next window widens for a while, as in the show. Locked until your first story Black Flash in Act III.

Partners with their own moves

Megumi's Divine Dogs track the next tell. Nobara's resonance keeps hurting. Maki breaks armor. Panda hits hard. Toge's "Don't move" skips a turn at a cost to his throat. Nanami exposes the 7:3 weak point. Todo's clap swaps places to dodge.

Last Light, your one technique

Stillness, renamed. In the alley you wanted the second before the dark to stop, and it did: Last Light holds the instant the lights went out. One technique means all the effort goes into making it look and feel right. When you use it, colour drains from the screen, dust hangs in the air, the curse freezes mid-motion, and one warm light stays on. It gains a move at each tier, ending in "The Second That Did Not Happen". Simple Domain (from Miwa) counters sure-hit attacks. Domain Expansion stays a Gojo set piece in Arc One.

Enemies that behave

Grade changes how a curse fights, not just its numbers: higher grades telegraph over two turns and change phase. Bosses get their own rules: Mahito's touch, Hanami's roots, Eso's poison, Todo's swaps.

Everyone has their own stats

Every character gets their own grade, cursed energy reserve, control and output, and grows on their own schedule whether you're there or not.

  • Train or take missions with someone and you both grow faster. That's the reason to pick a partner, not just their bond.
  • Fall behind and they grade up without you. Megumi or Nobara can be promoted before you, take jobs you can't, and say so. The journal shows it.
  • Grade is who gets picked. The Kyoto team and the big missions go to whoever has earned it.
  • The gap to the top is visible. Special grades don't fit on the same scale: Gojo's reserve runs off the edge of the bar. A spar with him is a lesson, not a fight.
  • Some break the rules. Maki has almost no cursed energy; her Heavenly Restriction puts it all into her body instead.

On screen

Hit flashes, screen shake, damage numbers, Last Light's grey-out and its one warm light, curses that move, a cut-in when you use a technique (your hands, in first person), black lightning for Black Flash, and battle music.

Worn talismans

Besides the one tool in your hand, you wear talismans: paper wards tucked into your uniform that change how you fight. They're the build choice between missions, and they're graded like sorcerers and curses, from Grade 4 up to Special grade.

  • Slots grow with your grade: one at Grade 4, two at Grade 3, three at Grade 2, four as a Grade 1 candidate.
  • You can wear talismans up to your own grade. Higher ones sit sealed in your inventory until you're promoted, so a lucky find is also a goal.
  • Special-grade talismans are the exception. They're legendary, one of a kind, and bind to you through a story moment rather than a promotion. You can only wear one, and each carries a heavy price.
  • Every strong ward has a price, so choosing them means something. A bigger reserve might mean slower recovery.
  • Where they come from: Utahime writes the lower grades (her own technique boosts other sorcerers' cursed energy, so it fits her), Mei Mei sells the rare ones, missions drop a few, and the legendary ones come from the story.
